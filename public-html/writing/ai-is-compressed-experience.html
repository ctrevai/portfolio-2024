AI Is Compressed Experience

The question isn't whether AI will replace your skills. It's what you would build if experience stopped being the bottleneck.

I spent seven years at The University of Tokyo learning robotics the slow way. Research is mostly years of reading, failing and slowly building intuition for what works. That intuition is what people mean by experience: patterns you can only earn by doing the thing many times.

Here's what has changed. The patterns a senior engineer picks up over a decade, the checklist a lawyer refines over a career, the instincts an operator builds by running a business through a few bad years: a large share of that is now available in a conversation. AI is compressed experience. It packs other people's lessons into something you can borrow on demand.

Experience used to be the bottleneck. Now the bottleneck is acting on it.

What compression gives you

It changes the math on "it's too late to learn that." The distance between beginner and useful used to be measured in years. For a lot of skills it's now months, sometimes weeks. You can ask the questions you'd be embarrassed to ask a colleague, get a first draft of the thing you don't know how to start, and have your mistakes explained to you while you make them.

That matters most for people who assumed a door had closed: the engineer who always wanted to start a business, the operator who wants to build software, the parent who wants to make a game with their kid. The prerequisite used to be years of preparation. Increasingly, the prerequisite is starting.

What compression doesn't give you

Compression is not judgment. AI will happily give you ten ways to do something. Knowing which one fits your situation, your customers and your constraints is still your job. So is knowing what good looks like, and so is the unglamorous work of finishing.

That's why I don't think AI makes experience worthless. It makes experience portable, and it makes judgment the scarce skill. If you've spent twenty years in an industry, you're not starting over. You're the person best placed to ask AI the right questions and spot the wrong answers.

The better question

Most conversations about AI and work start with fear: which jobs go away, which skills stop mattering. I'd rather start somewhere else. If experience stopped being the thing standing between you and the work you actually care about, what would you build?

Pick the thing you've put off because you "didn't have the background." Start this week, with AI as your senior colleague. You bring the passion and the judgment. Let it bring the years.
